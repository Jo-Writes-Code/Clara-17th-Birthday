# Design direction: Quiet love letter

## Three stylistic approaches

### Theme Name: Quiet love letter
Very editorial, intimate, and tactile: cream paper space, oxblood gestures, and a personal letter as the primary interface. The page should feel like opening something made by hand.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Midnight cinema
A darker, cinematic scrapbook with burgundy shadows, film stills, and small captions that feel like a private archive. Emotional and slightly more dramatic.
Probability: 0.02

### Theme Name: Garden party postcard
Airy and botanical with sun-faded reds, linen textures, and a loose postcard composition. Softer, brighter, and more playful while keeping the romance understated.
Probability: 0.04

## Chosen approach: Quiet love letter

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial romanticism with references to art-directed stationery, independent cinema title cards, and tactile love letters.

### Core Principles
1. **Personal before polished.** Small, specific phrases and imperfect handwritten accents carry more emotion than decorative excess.
2. **Asymmetry creates intimacy.** Each slide uses an offset composition, marginal notes, or cropped imagery rather than a generic centered layout.
3. **Cream is the canvas; red is the gesture.** Dark red only appears where the eye should pause: a button, a stamp, a rule, a title, or the final message.
4. **Motion stays quiet.** Transitions feel like paper sliding or a page turning, never like a loud celebration template.

### Color Philosophy
The background is warm paper (#f5efe6) rather than pure white so the whole site feels held and human. Oxblood (#7f1f2d) is used as the signature mark of affection, while ink (#2a2020) keeps long text soft and readable. Muted blush and olive details prevent the red from feeling heavy.

### Layout Paradigm
Full-screen horizontal pages with a persistent, minimal navigation rail. Content drifts between left-led letters, right-led memory cards, and narrow marginal notes. The rhythm should feel like moving through a small editorial booklet.

### Signature Elements
1. A tiny flower mark paired with a low-key “C / C” wordmark.
2. Handwritten red annotations and underlines that punctuate otherwise elegant serif typography.
3. Small index labels such as “03 / 10 · the little archive” to make the story feel collected.

### Interaction Philosophy
Every click should feel like opening a flap, turning a page, or discovering a private aside. Buttons are direct and tactile, with quick press feedback; quiz completion is celebrated quietly with a check mark and a one-line response.

### Animation
Slides move with a 520ms horizontal ease-out transition. Inside a slide, headings fade in with a 140ms lift and image cards reveal with a 40ms stagger. Buttons use a short scale-down on press. The “dont click” reveal uses a 220ms opacity and translate transition. All non-essential animation is disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Typography System
Display: Cormorant Garamond, with large light serif headlines and italic emphasis. Body: DM Sans for clear UI labels and supporting copy. Accent: Caveat for the handwritten letter, notes, and small personal asides. Headlines use generous line-height and occasional lowercase styling to keep the tone personal rather than formal.

### Brand Essence
An intimate birthday letter turned into a tiny interactive world for Clara — personal, cinematic, and made for one person. Personality: **tender, observant, quietly playful**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are simple, specific, and lowercase when they are meant to feel spoken. CTAs are short and lightly teasing. Microcopy sounds like a private note, not a marketing page.

Example lines: “for the girl who makes the ordinary feel like a good story.” / “there’s a tiny rule here. obviously you’ll break it.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use a compact hand-drawn hibiscus-and-lily emblem beside the monogram “C / C”. The mark should be recognizable without relying on a default text wordmark.

### Signature Brand Color
Oxblood red — `#7f1f2d` — a warm, deep red that reads as affection and ink rather than bright party decoration.
